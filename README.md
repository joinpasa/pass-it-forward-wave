Section 3: The Story (Origin + Problem)
Eyebrow: Where It Started

Headline: Born from Disaster. Built on Love.

Callout box (above the fold of this section): "Social isolation and loneliness have serious impacts on health and mortality… We must treat social health with the same urgency as physical and mental health." — WHO Commission on Social Connection, 2025

Body:

After Hurricane María devastated Puerto Rico, neighbors just showed up. They fixed roofs, shared food, held each other together in the dark. When Fiona hit five years later, the same thing happened. That wasn't coincidence. It was a revelation: when people are given a simple structure for kindness, they don't just participate. They ignite.

Pásalo Pa'lante ("Pass It Forward") channels the instinct that already lives in every community — the impulse to help, to connect, to show up — into something structured, visible, and contagious. Three steps. No cost. No prerequisites. Just the decision to do one kind thing and pass it on.

In 2025, Puerto Rico put this to the test. Over a million acts of kindness in five days. All 78 municipalities united. A certified world record. And something harder to quantify: a shift in how an entire population felt about each other and about themselves.

One in four adults worldwide report feeling lonely. Communities everywhere are fraying. But the data shows that coordinated kindness isn't just a nice idea — it's a measurable intervention. And it's ready to scale.

Pásalo Pa'lante isn't asking you to fix everything. It's inviting you into a wave of people who show up for each other. Your single act joins a million others.

Section 4: How It Works
Eyebrow: How It Works

Headline: Three Steps. A Million Ripples.

Subtitle: Whether you're one person or a thousand, the model is the same. Simple enough for a child. Powerful enough to break a world record.

Step 1: Do an Act of Kindness Big or small, it all counts. Buy someone coffee. Help a neighbor carry groceries. Listen to a friend who's struggling. Write a note of gratitude to someone who changed your life. Leave an encouraging message where a stranger will find it. There are no rules — just intention.

Step 2: Pass Forward a Kindness Card After your act, hand the person a Pásalo Pa'lante card (digital or physical) and invite them to keep the wave going. Each card connects back to the movement, so the chain never breaks and every act gets counted.

Step 3: Watch It Ripple This is where it gets powerful — and where the science backs it up. Research from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute shows that when just 10% of a population commits to a shared belief, it becomes unstoppable. In Puerto Rico, 35% of the population participated — exceeding that threshold by more than triple. One act becomes two. Two become twenty. Twenty become a wave that circles the globe.
